Part 1: Getting Started

What Is Racers & Rivals?

Racers & Rivals is a tabletop roleplaying game about training and racing. Inspired by Umamusume Pretty Derby and D&D 5th edition, the game follows the racer you create as they train their stats, build friendships with other players and NPCs, and pick up skills to prepare for their next race. On race days, racers run the track round by round, spending stamina to dash and activating skills to gain ground on their opponents. After each race, racers perform in a concert to gain fans.

The Typical Game Loop

  • Train. Training happens in segments called training turns (typically biweekly but ultimately up to GM discretion). On each turn a racer trains one main stat as well as one or more side stats depending on the type of training done.
  • Race. On certain training turns, races are held. Players can choose to run in a race, gaining stats and fans depending on how well they do at the cost of training a stat on that turn. Racers can run the race in full or skip it with a single roll against other runners or against a GM set DC.
  • Perform. After a race, racers perform in a concert and gain fans, items, and other bonuses that help them in future training turns to prepare for more races.

Roles

The Player

The players role is to create a character sheet and manage that sheet throughout the campaign. They are responsible for choosing what to train each turn and how their character wants to run in races as well as their characters motivations and the basis for their characters role in the story.

The Game Master

The Game Master (GM) sets the DC for checks, grants the players skills, items, or new abilities at their discretion, and decides how the general story of the game is to play out. The GM also runs NPCs.

Dice and Core Conventions

  • Dice: Training checks are rolled on a d100 and most other checks use a d20.
  • Units: Distances and speeds are measured in feet (ft). Races are run in rounds lasting roughly 20 seconds and (Unless the GM decides otherwise) shouldn’t last more than 10 rounds.
  • Proficiency Bonus: PB stands for proficiency bonus and is the racers innate skill. A racer's proficiency bonus starts at 2 and increases as their stats grow (see Part 5).
  • Stat Modifiers and advantage: A racer's stat modifiers come from their stat scores and increase by 1 for every two points gained in a stat. Some d20 rolls require the player or GM to roll at advantage or disadvantage which means you roll 2d20 and take the higher result in advantage and the lower result in disadvantage.

Part 2: Character Creation

Step-by-Step Creation

  • Assign stats. You gain 14 points to put into any stats. All of your stats start out at 5 and go to a maximum of 54 but no stat can be higher than 12 until after character creation.
  • Choose hobbies. You gain proficiency in any 4 hobbies of your choice.

Stat Ranks and Modifiers

Each stat has a score, and each score has a letter rank from G up through the U and L tiers. The full table of ranks and scores is in Appendix A.

Your proficiency bonus goes up every 50 total stat levels your character has.

Hobbies

Hobbies are interests your racer is proficient in. You choose 4 during character creation and will use them in roleplay situations to determine the result of certain actions. If you are proficient in a hobby, your proficiency bonus is added to the d20 check result. Each hobby is also tied to a stat, shown in brackets next to its name. If your bonus in that stat is positive, it is added to the d20 check result as well, whether or not you are proficient in the hobby. The full list of hobbies is in Appendix B

Part 3: Training

The Training Turn

Training takes place in whatever time frame the GM decides on (typically biweekly segments), and you can choose to train one stat per segment. Each training turn follows these steps.

  • Choose a stat. Pick the stat you want to train. You may also choose to train with another player or NPC if they are available (see Friendship and Team Training).
  • Make the encounter check. Roll a d20 plus the modifier of the stat you are training to see what happens during the training. Events can add bonuses or reductions to your training roll. (an encounter table is recommended but not mandatory and what the table consists of is completely up to the GM)

If the player rolls a total of 3 or lower on the encounter check for Speed, Power, Stamina, Guts, or a total of 2 or lower on Popularity, they must roll a d100, gaining the practice poor condition if they roll a 32 or lower. They will also get the practice poor condition if they roll a natural 1 on the d20 as well as automatically failing the training check (on a Wit check, a natural 1 only automatically fails the training check and does not give practice poor).

If the player gets a natural 20 on the d20 they automatically succeed on the training check and gain the practice perfect condition. On a Wit check they gain fast learner instead, and on a Popularity check they gain hot topic instead.

  • Make the training check. Roll a d100 against the training DC, applying any bonuses or reductions gained from the event. A natural 100 automatically succeeds and doubles the stat progress you gain.
  • Apply stat progress. On a success you gain stat progress. On a failure you lose some. You also gain progress in side stats.
  • Raise the training DC. Your next training DC increases by 10 (or 20 if you trained with a friend).

Example of Play: Flash Step decides to train speed for the turn and rolls a 6 on the d20. Adding her -3 speed modifier shows that the total result is a 3. Because of this, Flash Step has to roll a d100 and ends up getting a 27, giving her the practice poor condition.

Training DC

  • Rising DC: The base training DC is 0. Each time you train, the DC of your next training check increases by 10, to a maximum of 100.
  • Resting: Resting reduces the DC by 10 times 1d10 and costs a training slot.
  • Training Wit: Training Wit reduces the DC by 10 (20 if you trained with a friend) whether you succeed or fail.

Stat Progress and Training Level

  • Main stat: When you succeed, you gain stat progress equal to 2 times your training level. When you fail, you lose half as much.
  • Side stats: You also gain half of the base stat progress from the main stat (before any friendship bonus) as stat progress in that training's side stat(s). You lose nothing in side stats if you fail.
  • Converting progress: Every 10 stat progress points equal 1 point in that stats score.
  • Training level: Your training level increases by 1 every 4 points you gain in a stat from training that stat, up to a maximum training level of 5. Progress gained in side stats does not count toward your training level.

Training Reference. Speed trains Speed and Power. Stamina trains Stamina and Guts. Power trains Power and Stamina. Guts trains Guts, Power, and Speed. Wit trains Wit and Speed. Popularity trains Popularity and Guts.

Friendship

Friendship level shows how close you are with a player or NPC. You raise it by training with them or by spending time with them through roleplay. When you choose to train with someone, you gain extra benefits based on your friendship level, but your training DC increases by 20 instead of 10 after the training. On a success, the stat progress bonus from your friendship level is added to your main stat after the base main stat and side stat progress has been calculated, so it is never doubled and is not shared with side stats.

Team Training

You can train with more than one player or NPC. When you do, everyone works together to grow stronger.

  • Choose a leader. One participant is the leader for the training roll. Everyone attempts to beat the leader's training DC, plus 10 for each additional person participating.
  • Roll encounter checks. Everyone rolls an encounter check. The results are added together, and the resulting effect applies to everyone.

(can be omitted if the GM doesn’t have an encounter table)

  • Roll training checks. Everyone rolls a training check against the leader's training DC. Each participant who succeeds adds +10 to the leader's training check result.
  • Award progress. If the leader succeeds, everyone gains stat progress equal to (2 times training level + 1 for each additional person participating).
  • Spend energy. Everyone participating raises their next training DC by 20, similar to friendship training.

Friendship in a Team. Friendship mechanics apply as normal, but the training DC reduction only applies from the leader's friendship level.

Part 4: Racing

Before the Race

On certain training turns, races are held that you can run in. You can run the race in full or skip it.

Skipping a Race

If you are the only one racing, you can choose to just view the result by rolling a d20 and adding your highest ability score modifier to beat a DC decided by the GM. If multiple racers are skipping the race, the racer with the highest result that beats the DC wins.

The Track

Racers start on a starting (Green) square or a starting line. You cannot activate skills within 10 ft of the starting square/line. (track length, type, and shape is completely up to GM discretion)

Running the Race

If you are running the race in full, start by rolling initiative. Players take their turns in initiative order, and the goal is to reach the finish line in the fewest number of rounds. During their turns, racers use the speed and dash mechanics, manage stamina, and activate skills.

Turn Summary. On your turn: (1) Decide whether to dash (spend 1 stamina). (2) Activate skills with Wit checks. (3) Move your current speed. (4) Make any passing saves that apply.

Speed, Dash, and Stamina

Speed

Each racer's base speed is 40 by default and can be increased by certain skills. Your maximum movement speed is 10 times your Speed score. This is how fast you can run, and you must build up to it.

Dashing

When you choose to dash, you spend 1 stamina and gain additional movement equal to 40 plus 5 times your Power modifier. The additional speed lasts until the end of your next turn. If you fail to dash before the end of your next turn, you lose speed equal to 40 plus 5 times your Power modifier each turn until you are back at base speed or you dash again.

This lets you dash repeatedly to build up to your maximum speed, dash every other turn to stay above base speed without accelerating, or return to base speed after a few turns of deceleration.

Stamina and Exhaustion

  • Stamina pool: Each racer has a stamina pool equal to their Stamina modifier (minimum 1), used to dash.
  • Pushing past your limits: You cannot dash with no stamina unless you succeed on a Guts save made with advantage (roll 2d20 and keep the higher, DC 10 plus your current exhaustion level). If you do, you push past your limits and take a level of exhaustion. A natural 20 on this Guts check always succeeds and a natural 1 always fails.
  • Exhaustion levels 1 to 5: Your dash speed is reduced by 5 ft and you suffer a -2 to all d20 checks for every level of exhaustion. This only reduces dash speed gained (not speed lost) and counts on the turn you gain the exhaustion.
  • Exhaustion level 6: Everything plays out the same as levels 1-5, but at the end of the round you fall unconscious and are in critical condition for the next 6 training turns after the race. For each of those turns, roll a d20. On an 11 or higher, reduce the remaining turns in critical condition by 1. (The specifics of what happens is up to the player and GM)
  • Recovery: You regain all your stamina on a long rest (2 hours of light activity and 6 hours of sleep) and half on a short rest (4 hours of light activity) with a minimum 1. You also recover from all levels of exhaustion on a long rest.

Passing Saves and Terrain

Some skills involve passing saves to resist an effect while other skills grant bonuses to them. Skills that hinder other runners usually force a passing save against DC of 10 plus the activator's proficiency bonus which must be beaten by rolling a d20 and adding your power modifier to the result. Some skills also create difficult terrain, which halves the racers movement speed while they are in the area.

Finishing and Tiebreakers

The racer who reaches the finish line in the fewest rounds wins. If two racers cross the finish line in the same round, they roll Guts (Tiebreaker) checks and add the result to their race initiative. The racer with the higher total wins.

Rewards

  • Finishing Bonus: Completing a race gives +2 stat progress to all of your stats. (+1 if you don’t finish the race completely)
  • Race grade bonus: G3 (+3), G2 (+4), or G1 (+5) stat progress on top of the finishing bonus.
  • Training DC after a race: After a race, your Training DC goes up by 10 plus 10 times half your exhaustion level (rounded down).
  • Placement bonus: 3rd place (+1), 2nd place (+2), or 1st place (+3) stat progress on top of the other two bonuses.

Example of Play: After rolling initiative, Flash Step goes first with an 18 while Staying Strong goes next with a 16. Flash step starts their turn by dashing, increasing their current speed from 40 to 80. Knowing that they will lose the extra speed at the end of their next turn, they decide to save their stamina to dash next turn instead of dashing again. Next in initiative is Staying Strong who has plenty of stamina but lacks the speed to openly challenge Flash Step, so they decide to dash this turn to put pressure on Flash Step hoping to scare them into exhausting themselves while not pushing that far. And with a 30 to their dash speed, Staying strong moves 70 total feet getting 10ft behind Flash Step.

Part 5: Wit and Skills

Using Skills

  • Activation: On your turn you may attempt to activate a number of skills equal to your proficiency bonus. Each attempt is a Wit check (DC 10 plus the skill's level). A natural 20 always succeeds and a natural 1 always fails.
  • Uses: Attempting to activate a skill uses one of that skill's uses whether you succeed or fail. The same skill can be used a number of times per race equal to half your proficiency bonus (rounded down, minimum 1).
  • Duration: A skill that is not instantaneous lasts a number of rounds equal to your proficiency bonus. Unless stated otherwise, the effect ends at the end of your turn on the final round. Using a skill that is already in effect does not stack.
  • Categories: Skills can influence speed, stamina, dashing, and mental awareness, and can grant racing maneuvers. They are organized into levels 1 to 8, where higher levels are more powerful and harder to activate.

Skill Levels and Proficiency

You can learn skills through training or at the GM's discretion. The maximum level of skill you can learn equals your proficiency bonus. Your proficiency bonus starts at 2 and goes up every 50 total stat levels your character has.

Unique Skills and Green Skills

  • Unique skills: Stronger skills that players design for themselves to represent their racing style and personality. They are unlocked by story progression. You can use only one unique skill per race, and you must make a Wit check (DC 10) to activate it. The use that unlocks it is free.
  • Green skills: Passive skills that automatically activate at the start of a race.

Part 6: Concerts and Fans

The Concert

After a race, you perform in a concert by rolling a d20 plus your popularity modifier and proficiency bonus to see how well you did. Doing well can give you things like items and positive status conditions.

Fans

You gain fans equal to 10 times your concert result, plus an additional 20 (3rd), 40 (2nd), and 60 (1st) fans for the top 3 racers. Fans can be doubled by the Hot Topic condition and by Glow Sticks, and the Awe-Inspiring Spectacle green skill grows stronger with your fan count.

Example of Play: After a difficult race, it’s time for Flash Step to perform for their fans. They got a 17 on their performance check gaining 170 fans and because they did well, the GM decided that one of the fans threw up a vita 20 that Flash Step gets to keep for future training.

Appendix C: Quick Reference

Training Turn

  • Choose a stat to train (optionally with friends).
  • Roll the encounter check.
  • Roll d100 against the training DC.
  • Success: gain 2 x training level progress (a natural 100 automatically succeeds and doubles it). Failure: lose half as much.
  • Gain half progress in side stats (no loss on failure), then add any friendship progress bonus to the main stat.
  • Raise the training DC by 10 (20 with a friend), max 100.

Race Turn

  • Roll initiative at the start of the race.
  • On your turn, choose whether to dash (1 stamina, +40 + 5 x Power modifier).
  • Attempt skills (Wit check, DC 10 + skill level), up to your proficiency bonus per turn.
  • Move at your current speed (base 40, up to 10 x Speed score).
  • Make passing saves if a skill requires it.
  • First to the finish line in the fewest rounds wins. Ties are broken by Guts.
